# Approved SOP: Emergency Shutdown Summary

Emergency shutdown is used when continued operation may cause injury, fire, equipment damage, major spill, or uncontrolled release of energy.

## When Emergency Shutdown May Be Needed

- A person is trapped, injured, or in immediate danger.
- Fire, smoke, electrical arcing, or burning smell is observed.
- A guard, interlock, or safety device fails during operation.
- A machine runs abnormally and cannot be controlled by normal stop.
- A major leak, spill, pressure release, or unusual vibration occurs.

## High-Level Procedure

1. Press the nearest emergency stop if there is immediate danger and it is safe to reach.
2. Warn nearby employees and move away from the hazard area.
3. Notify the supervisor, control room, or emergency response contact according to the site escalation procedure.
4. Do not restart equipment after an emergency stop until the responsible supervisor and maintenance or safety personnel have completed the required checks.
5. Preserve the scene when required for incident investigation, unless doing so would create additional danger.
6. Follow evacuation, first aid, spill response, or fire response instructions posted for the area.

## Important Boundary

This summary explains emergency shutdown concepts. Actual emergency actions must follow site-specific procedures, posted instructions, alarms, and trained emergency response directions.
